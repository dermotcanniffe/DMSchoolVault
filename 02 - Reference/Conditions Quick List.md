# 🌀 Conditions Quick List

⬅ [[02 - Reference/Reference Home|Reference Home]]

When a rule says a creature is "poisoned" or "prone", it means a specific thing. Here's the short version so you can rule fast at the table. (Plain-language summaries; check the full rules for edge cases.)

| Condition | What it means in play |
|-----------|-----------------------|
| **Blinded** | Can't see. Fails sight-based checks. Attacks *against* it have advantage; its attacks have disadvantage. |
| **Charmed** | Can't attack the charmer; charmer has advantage on social checks with it. |
| **Deafened** | Can't hear. Fails hearing-based checks. |
| **Frightened** | Disadvantage on rolls while it can see the source of fear; can't willingly move closer to it. |
| **Grappled** | Speed 0; can't move. Ends if the grappler is incapacitated or they're pulled apart. |
| **Incapacitated** | Can't take actions or bonus actions. |
| **Invisible** | Can't be seen without help. Attacks against it have disadvantage; its attacks have advantage. |
| **Paralyzed** | Can't move or act, auto-fails STR/DEX saves, attacks against it have advantage, and hits from within 5 ft are **critical hits**. Nasty. |
| **Petrified** | Turned to stone. Out of the fight, resists most damage, unaware of surroundings. |
| **Poisoned** | Disadvantage on attack rolls and ability checks. Very common. |
| **Prone** | Knocked down. Moving costs extra; disadvantage on its attacks; melee attacks against it have advantage, ranged have disadvantage. Standing up costs half its movement. |
| **Restrained** | Speed 0; attacks against it have advantage, its attacks have disadvantage; disadvantage on DEX saves. |
| **Stunned** | Can't move, barely acts, auto-fails STR/DEX saves, attacks against it have advantage. |
| **Unconscious** | Down and unaware; drops everything, falls prone, auto-fails STR/DEX saves, hits from within 5 ft crit. |

> [!tip] The recurring pattern
> Most "bad" conditions do some mix of: **attacks against you get advantage**, **your attacks get disadvantage**, and **you can't move/act**. If you forget the exact wording, ruling in that spirit is close enough to keep the game moving.

## Exhaustion (the stacking one)
Exhaustion builds in **levels** (1–6). Roughly: each level makes things worse, and level 6 is death. You rarely need it; if it comes up, look up the exact table. For most tables, "you're tired, take disadvantage" covers level 1–2 fine.
