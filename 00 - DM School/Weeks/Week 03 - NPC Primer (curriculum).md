## **Point of View**

1. Define NPC Point of View  
   1. A point of view about other people or life (as opposed to things, or if starts as a point of view about objects, include a why and make it apply to a more macro view)  
   2. This is a jumping off point so you can easily begin a scene using this NPC, whether they wind up doubling down on the POV, completely forgetting it, or shifting it (based on where the PCs take the scene), so don’t be too precious with it. It doesn’t have to be a deep philosophical exploration of humanity, but it can).  
   3. Many bonds, ideals, and/or flaws are really great POVs, and NPCs generally need only one  
   4. What does a person think and why? That’s a point of view.  
   5. Use people you know as examples if you can’t think of something.  
   6. Use your own POVs about the world if you can’t think of something.  
   7. Archetypes and simplicity are just fine, remember, you are providing a way in for PC/main character energy to thrive.  
   8. To get into it quickly, ask:  
      1. Does your NPC generally like or dislike the situation they’re in?  
      2. Does your NPC generally like or dislike strangers aka the PCs?  
   9. Ex. Going on adventures is my love language; The entire world is out to get me, even you\!;  They’re always watching; I’m the baby…

# **Exercise One: Low Prep NPC Improv**

## **Prepare**

Write out one NPC and [add it to this DM list](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1zhfXl818A34E5x5ugzNcz3knF3TEDGE9ZiI2OuMaJvE/edit?gid=0#gid=0) (anywhere, bottom or top)

You’re the DM playing this NPC. 

1. Decide “what’s going on” / “what’s really going on”  
2. Decide an environment  
   1. Maybe today’s the day when  
2. Define NPC Point of View  
3. Define what NPC wants (objective)  
4. Write out 3 bits of knowledge about this NPC, other NPCs, or the world around this NPC about “what’s going on.”   
   1. *These can be your decor that you have defined already, or you can make something up right now*  
   2. Ex. Gegangen is a high level druid who is beloved by the Erndker Fathom’s monarch, an ancient grotesque seaspawn herself (more octopus than anything else)  
   3. Ex. He is the Tidekeeper and can predict and manage the currents in Erndker Fathom. But he also is an expert on portals, planes, and planejumping  
   4. Ex. Works closely with the Monarch Xu (zhu), but is on the side of The Obligation (The Obligation–faction whose purpose is to prevent putting the articles back together)  
5. One “trigger” that could turn the NPC from friendly to unfriendly (or vice versa) and what would happen if they do that.  
   1. ***You are looking for trust to be established, deepened, or broken***  
   2. Ex. IF GEGANGEN FINDS OUT THE PCs HAVE THE CHALICE, THIS IS A WHOLE DIFFERENT BALL GAME–they are taken to the prison, where they will be readied and presented as gifts to Lux’ir.

## **Begin**

In this class, half will be PCs and half will be DMs. We won’t switch. For DMs, do the following, and for PCs, your NPC is now a PC.

1. **Give the player a character objective**. *Here are some good adventure/non-interpersonal ones:*  
   1. Get something/someone  
   2. Save (Rescue) something/someone  
   3. Kill (Destroy/Imprison) something/someone  
   4. Discover something/someone  
   5. \*\*Achieve something\*\* (use sparingly) or use it attached to “in order to \_\_\_ \[insert a more active verb from above\]  
   6. \*\*Investigate something\*\* (use sparingly) or use it attached to “in order to \_\_\_ \[insert a more active verb from above\]  
2. Describe the environment and what the player needs to know quickly, and get to the NPC ASAP–the NPC will be the “challenge” in the way of the PC getting their objective  
   3. ex. You enter the witch’s hut with loads of bottles and ingredients covering the shelves. XXXX notices you and… \[begin scene\]  
6. Go for it (oh what the fuck, go for it anyway)\!

\*\*no more POVs about divinity-based magic users and commenting on religion  
\*\*If a wrong/bad guy comes up or an argument, default DM is wrong/bad guy, and bonus points if they meant to do it and will do it again.

## **Option One: NPC Improv–The PC knows the NPC and it’s a surprise**

1. DM gives the PC a character objective–why they are in this specific location and what they hope to learn/gain from \*anyone\* in this location, and this NPC happens to be the first one they run across. Can start in action.  
   1. Ex. The PC needs to find and take down a dark potion-smuggling gang called the Shards of Night  
2. DM chooses an NPC stat block from SRD pdf pages 395-403 (or [here](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/br-2024/how-to-use-a-monster) and click “NPC Descriptions” and plans out a real character. \*\*Remember, you can change race and flavor text at will\!\*\* This time, you will commit to:  
   1. A race/gender  
      1. Ex. female elf  
   2. A point of view & why  
      1. Ex. I’m on my own–if it’s getting done, it’s up to me and only me.  
   3. Define what NPC wants (objective)  
   4. Do they like/not like the PC & why  
      1. Ex. They do not like the PC because they view them as having abandoned the family when they set off to adventure  
   5. One “trigger” that could turn the NPC from friendly to unfriendly (or vice versa) and what would happen if they do that.  
      1. ***You are looking for trust to be established, deepened, or broken***  
   6. A “moment before” –what they were doing and their emotional state as a result of what they’re doing before the PCs show up  
      1. Ex. Reforging her wedding ring  
   7. How they know you, the PC–it’s been a while since they’ve seen you and it’s a surprise the PC is here (parent, sibling, friend, enemy, etc).  
      1. Ex. the PC’s elven mother who is going through a rough time with the PC’S father.  
   8. Three bits of info the NPC has that the PC does not have. The PC may or may not want this information. ONE PIECE of information should pertain to the PC’s objective.  
      1. Ex. 1\. Sergeant Tuppy’s baked rolls are better than mine, and I’ll never elevate myself in society. 2\. Your father and I are getting a divorce. 3\. The Shards of Night cult are rumored to be in town, and have a makeshift HQ in the cemetery.  
3. DM chooses a fantasy geography from their own list or from the [collective](https://docs.google.com/document/d/19GN4nqw8y1chV_sNjynwjbww6cGrnygdM1e1RMfEI2E/edit?usp=sharing) in which this NPC can exist.  
   1. Ex. Moonlit treehouse.

\*If you choose this one, it can be a positive or negative reunion, I don’t care.  
\*If it is negative, the DM default is that you are the bad guy, you did a bad thing, and even though maybe you have justification, you definitely did the bad thing. Bonus points if you’d do it again.

## **Option Two: Monster Improv–They know you’re trying to slay them, and they don’t want you to**

1. DM gives the PC a character objective–to slay the monster. But why?  
   1. Ex. The monster is scaring people from leaving their homes, hurting the economy.  
2. DM plans out a full talking monster (disregard language issues, everyone can speak Common) \*\*Remember, you can change race and flavor text at will\!\*\*  This time, you will commit to:  
   1. The creature and its gender  
      1. Ex. Nonbinary Shrieker (Fungi, SRD p309 or [here](https://www.dndbeyond.com/monsters/17013-shrieker))  
   2. A point of view & why  
      1. Ex. Existence is terrifying.  
   3. Define what NPC wants (objective)  
   4. Do they like/not like the PC & why  
      1. Ex. They do not like the PC because they are convinced the PC will kill them  
   5. One “trigger” that could turn the NPC from friendly to unfriendly (or vice versa) and what would happen if they do that.  
      1. ***You are looking for trust to be established, deepened, or broken***  
   6. A “moment before” –what they were doing and their emotional state as a result of what they’re doing before the PCs show up  
      1. Ex. Practicing calming mantras  
   7. If there is a way to relate this to the PC, do so, but not necessary.  
   8. Three bits of info the NPC has that the PC does not have. The PC may or may not want this information. At least ONE PIECE of information should pertain to the PC’s objective, and should be WHY the PCs should not kill them.  
      1. Ex. 1\. I am the result of a spore caught on the wind, totally alone. 2\. The town is less disturbing than the prowling black panther on the other side of my range. 3\. I scream because I’m terrified but every time I do, children run back home and the black panther doesn’t get them.  
3. DM chooses a fantasy geography from their own list or from the [collective](https://docs.google.com/document/d/19GN4nqw8y1chV_sNjynwjbww6cGrnygdM1e1RMfEI2E/edit?usp=sharing) in which this NPC can exist.  
   1. Ex. Bustling fantasy park with magic playground equipment in a metropolitan city.
